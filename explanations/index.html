From Article to Headline

A from-scratch walkthrough of the Seq2Seq title-generation project, from the first matrix multiplication to fine-tuned Transformers. Read in order: each page uses only ideas the pages before it have already built.

assignment_spec.pdf · Overview
"In this assignment, we will use Deep Neural Network (DNN) based language models to generate titles for new Wikipedia articles, given the body text. Specifically, we shall train a Sequence to Sequence (Seq2seq) model that encodes an input sequence of tokens and then generates a desired sequence of tokens, using Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) and Transformers. Such kinds of seq2seq models are useful for tasks like translation and summarization, and the "title-generation" task of this assignment can be considered as a form of extreme document compression. Finally, we shall try to improve different parts of the model for better performance."

Pages 1 and 2 build the prerequisites that sentence assumes. Every page after that opens with the assignment brief's own description of its part, explains it using only what came before, and says exactly what the notebook implemented.

  1. Prerequisite 1Neural network basics
    Vectors and dot products, layers, activations, loss, gradient descent, backpropagation, Adam, training/validation/test, dropout, weight decay, gradient clipping.
  2. Prerequisite 2Sequence models
    Tokens and embeddings, the chain rule of probability, softmax and cross-entropy (derived), RNNs and vanishing gradients, the GRU, the encoder–decoder and its bottleneck, teacher forcing, greedy decoding.
  3. Part APreprocessing
    The exact cleaning rule, a token-by-token worked example, why lemmatization, why spaCy batching was 3.8× faster, and what cleaning costs later.
  4. Part B1The basic RNN Seq2Seq model
    Vocabulary, every tensor shape and parameter count, one training step, mixed precision, ROUGE with worked examples, and four causes of the 0.25 score.
  5. Part B2Four upgrades to the RNN
    GloVe, the hierarchical encoder, the two-GRU decoder and beam search, each shown as the spec describes it and as the notebook implements it, plus what the runs can and can't show.
  6. Part C1Self-attention, and fine-tuning T5
    Subword tokens, attention from the dot product up, multi-head attention, position biases, masked and cross-attention, pretraining, and the fine-tuning run.
  7. Part C2Prompting Flan-T5, zero-shot
    Instruction tuning, zero-shot vs. few-shot vs. fine-tuning, the two prompts, and why a model never trained on this data tied with one that was.
  8. Wrap-upAll results, and what they support
    Every score with its measurement conditions, which comparisons are fair, the time for each part, and four conclusions.
